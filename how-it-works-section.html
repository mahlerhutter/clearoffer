How to write a "how it works" section that explains the next step before the click.

For freelancers, consultants and solo operators. Short answer: right next to your main button, say in three short steps what the visitor sends, when and how they pay, and what they receive and when. People hesitate when they can't tell whether a button means paying, filling in a form or getting pulled into a sales call. Below: why it matters, the three-step pattern, what each step must say, before/after rewrites, mistakes and a block you can copy.

Why the next step needs explaining

A button is a small commitment with an unknown cost. Before clicking, a visitor quietly asks: will this charge my card? Will I have to talk to someone? How long until I get something? If the page doesn't answer, the safe choice is not to click. A short "how it works" section removes that guesswork without adding a single sales sentence.

Rule of thumb: a visitor should be able to say what happens in the next ten minutes after clicking, before they click.

The three-step pattern

  1. What you send or do. The visitor's part: fill in a short form, book a slot, send a link or files.
  2. When and how you pay. Before or after delivery, by card, invoice or payment link, and whether anything is charged at the moment of clicking.
  3. What you receive and when. The deliverable and a timeframe with a clear starting point, such as "within three working days of payment".

Three steps is a guideline, not a law. If your process genuinely has a fourth step that the buyer notices (a kickoff call, an approval round), add it. If you need seven, the process itself is probably the problem.

What each step must say

  • Who acts. "You send…", "I review…", "You receive…". Mixing passive phrases like "the project is initiated" hides who has to do what.
  • A concrete verb and object. "Send the URL of your page", not "get in touch".
  • Time where it matters. At least the delivery step needs a timeframe you actually keep.
  • The money moment. If payment happens at the click, say so. If it doesn't, say that too: "No payment until you've seen the quote."
  • What happens if it isn't a fit. One line is enough: "If I can't help, I'll tell you within a day and you pay nothing." Only write this if it's true.

Before and after

Illustrative rewrites on made-up examples, not real clients.

Copywriter

Before: "Get in touch to start your project." After: "1. Fill in a five-question brief (about 10 minutes). 2. I reply within one working day with a fixed quote; nothing is charged until you accept it. 3. You get the first draft within five working days of paying the deposit."

Photographer

Before: "Book now." After: "1. Pick a date in the calendar. 2. Pay a €100 deposit to hold it; the rest is due on the day. 3. You receive 30 edited photos by download link within seven days of the shoot."

IT consultant

Before: "Request a consultation." After: "1. Book a free 20-minute call. 2. If we're a fit, you get a written scope and fixed price within two working days. 3. Work starts after you sign and pay the first invoice."

Notice what changed: every "after" names who acts, when money moves and when something arrives. None of them adds adjectives.

Common mistakes

  • Describing your internal process: "research, strategy, execution, optimisation" explains how you work, not what the buyer does next.
  • Hiding the payment step: buyers who discover a payment screen they didn't expect often leave there.
  • A button that does something else: if the steps say "fill in a form" but the button opens a calendar, trust drops at the exact moment you need it.
  • Putting it far from the button: a "how it works" section at the bottom doesn't help the person hovering over the button at the top. Put a one-line version next to every primary button.
  • Timeframes you don't keep: "within 24 hours" is a promise. Use a timeframe that's true on a busy week.
  • Icons without words: three nice icons with "Contact · Create · Deliver" under them say nothing.

Where it goes on the page

Put a one-line version directly under the main button ("Send your link, pay €…, get … within … days") and a short three-step section between the "what you get" block and the price (see describe your deliverable and the price section guide). The button wording should match step 1 (see the call to action guide), and the headline should already say what the visitor gets (see the headline guide).

Copy-paste "how it works" block

Replace the brackets. Delete any line that isn't true for you.

How it works
1. You [send / book / fill in]: [what exactly, and how long it takes]
2. You pay [€… / a deposit of €…] [before / after] [delivery / the quote] by [card / invoice / payment link]. [Nothing is charged when you click.]
3. You receive [the deliverable] within [N working days] of [payment / your brief].

[If it isn't a fit: what happens, only if true.]

One-line version under the button:
[Send …] · [pay €… upfront] · [get … within … days]

Quick self-check

  • Could a visitor say what happens in the ten minutes after clicking?
  • Is it clear whether clicking costs money?
  • Does every step say who acts?
  • Is there a delivery timeframe you actually keep?
  • Does the button do what step 1 says?
  • Is a one-line version visible next to the main button?
