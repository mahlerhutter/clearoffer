How to write a service page headline that says who it's for and what they get.

For freelancers, consultants and solo operators. Short answer: the first screen of your page has to name the buyer and the result they get, in words that buyer would use. If your headline could sit on any competitor's page unchanged, it isn't doing its job yet. Below: a simple formula, before/after rewrites, common mistakes, a quick test and a block you can copy.

What the headline is for

A visitor lands on your page and decides very quickly whether to keep reading. In that moment they are asking two things: "is this for someone like me?" and "what would I get?" The headline and the line under it should answer both before they scroll.

Rule of thumb: the headline is not where you describe yourself or your method. It is where the right visitor recognises themselves and the result they want.

A simple formula

[Result] for [who], [how fast or in what format].

  • Result: what the buyer has afterwards, not what you do. "A clear price page", not "copywriting services".
  • Who: a specific group you actually serve. "Independent physiotherapists", "solo B2B consultants", "small Shopify shops", not "businesses".
  • How fast or format: a delivery time or a concrete deliverable, if it's true and fixed. "In five working days", "as a one-page PDF".

You don't need all three in the headline itself. Put the result and the buyer in the headline, and the format, timeframe and price in the subheadline directly underneath.

Before and after

Illustrative rewrites on made-up examples, not real clients.

Web designer

Before: "Creative web design with passion." After: "A website that books appointments, for independent physiotherapists. Live in three weeks, fixed price."

Business consultant

Before: "Holistic consulting for sustainable growth." After: "A 90-day sales plan for solo B2B consultants, built with you in two workshops."

Bookkeeper

Before: "Your partner for numbers." After: "Monthly bookkeeping for freelancers in Austria, done by the 10th, so your tax advisor gets clean books."

Copywriter

Before: "Words that work." After: "Rewritten service page copy for coaches who get visits but no enquiries. One page, delivered in a week."

Notice what changed: each "after" names a buyer, a concrete result and a format or timeframe. None of them adds an adjective.

The line under the headline

The subheadline carries what didn't fit: the format, the timeframe, the price or starting price, and one reason to trust you. Keep it to one or two sentences.

  • Say what physically arrives (a document, a call, a file) and when.
  • If the price is fixed, putting it here saves the visitor a scroll (see the price section guide).
  • Follow it with one primary button (see the call to action guide), and, if you have one, a link to a sample (see proof without testimonials).

Common mistakes

  • Describing yourself: "passionate", "experienced", "your trusted partner". The visitor can't check any of it, and every competitor says the same.
  • Describing the method: "holistic", "data-driven", "agile". Methods matter later on the page, not in the first line.
  • No buyer at all: "for businesses" or "for everyone" means the right buyer doesn't recognise themselves.
  • A clever slogan instead of a sentence: wordplay can work for big brands people already know. For a new solo operator, plain beats clever.
  • Promises you can't keep: "guaranteed results", "double your revenue". Unbacked claims cost trust and can mislead buyers.
  • Industry jargon: use the words buyers use when they describe their problem to you, not the words your peers use.

The five-second test

  1. Show only the first screen of your page (on a phone, if most of your visitors are on phones) to someone who doesn't know your business.
  2. Take it away after about five seconds.
  3. Ask: "Who is this for?" and "What would you get?"
  4. If they can't answer both in their own words, rewrite the headline and subheadline and test again.

No one to ask? Cover your logo and read the headline next to two competitors' headlines. If you could swap them without anyone noticing, it's too generic.

Copy-paste first-screen block

Replace the brackets. Delete any part that isn't true for you.

Headline:     [Result the buyer gets] for [specific buyer]

Subheadline:  [What arrives: document / call / file], [delivered in / by when]. [Fixed price or "from €…"].

Button:       [Verb + outcome, e.g. "Book your audit"]     Link: [See a sample]

Quick self-check

  • Does the first screen name a specific buyer?
  • Does the headline state a result, not your personality or method?
  • Does the subheadline say what arrives and when?
  • Is every promise in it something you can actually deliver?
  • Would a stranger answer "who is it for?" and "what do I get?" after five seconds?
