Offer page checklist: 10 checks before you send traffic.

For freelancers, consultants and solo operators with one page that has to sell one thing. Open your page on your phone, go through the ten checks below, and fix whatever fails. Each check comes with a quick fix.

Start with the 10-second test

Show the page to someone who doesn't know your business (or read only what's visible without scrolling). After ten seconds they should be able to say three things:

  1. Who it is for.
  2. What they get (the outcome, not the process).
  3. What to do next (one button or link).

If any of the three is missing, fix checks 1–5 first. The rest won't matter much.

Message — checks 1 to 3

1 · The audience is named in the first screen

Fails when the headline could fit any business. Quick fix: put the buyer in the headline or the line under it, e.g. "for independent physiotherapists" instead of "for businesses".

2 · The headline states an outcome

Fails when it describes you ("passionate designer") or your method. Quick fix: rewrite as "[result] for [who] in [timeframe or format]". More detail: how to write a service page headline.

3 · One problem, in the buyer's words

Fails when the page lists five pains or uses industry jargon. Quick fix: pick the one problem buyers mention most and use their own phrasing (from emails, calls or reviews you actually received).

Offer and price — checks 4 and 5

4 · The deliverable is concrete

Fails on "tailored solutions" and "packages". Quick fix: say what physically arrives: a document, a call, a file, a number of revisions, a delivery time. More detail: how to describe your deliverable.

5 · The price (or a starting price) is visible

Fails when the visitor has to email to find out whether they can afford it. Quick fix: show a fixed price or a "from €…" next to the main CTA. If you really can't, show the range and what moves it. More detail: how to write the price section.

Action — checks 6 and 7

6 · One primary call to action

Fails when "Book a call", "Subscribe", "Follow us" and "Contact" compete. Quick fix: one verb + one outcome ("Get my report", "Book the audit"), repeated at the top and bottom. Everything else becomes a plain text link. More detail: one call to action or several.

7 · The next step is explained before the click

Fails when people don't know whether clicking means paying, a form, or a sales call. Quick fix: add a three-step "how it works" line right next to the button: what they send, when they pay, what they receive and when. More detail: how to write a "how it works" section.

Proof and trust — checks 8 and 9

8 · At least one piece of real proof

Fails when there is none, or worse, when it's vague or made up. Quick fix: use what you actually have: a sample deliverable, a before/after of your own work, a named past project you're allowed to mention, or a real number. A sample beats an anonymous quote. More detail: what proof to show when you have no testimonials yet.

9 · It's clear who is behind the page

Fails when there's no name, company, address or contact. In many EU countries an imprint is also a legal requirement for business sites. Quick fix: footer with legal name, address, a working contact email, and privacy/terms links.

Mechanics — check 10

10 · It works on a phone, end to end

Fails more often than people think. Quick fix: on your own phone, load the page on mobile data, tap the main button, and finish the whole path: the form sends, the payment link opens, the email address isn't misspelled, no link goes to a 404.

How to read your result

  • 9–10 passes: the page is unlikely to be the bottleneck. Look at traffic and who you are sending to it.
  • 6–8 passes: fix the failed checks in order (lower numbers first). Usually an afternoon of edits.
  • 5 or fewer: rewrite the first screen (checks 1, 2, 5, 6) before anything else.
