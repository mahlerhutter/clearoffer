How to show who is behind your service page, so buyers know who they're paying.

For freelancers, consultants and solo operators. Short answer: show your real name or company name, where you're based, a contact address that actually gets answered, and a legal notice (imprint) where your country requires one. Put a short "who you're dealing with" block near the price and the full details in the footer. Below: why it matters, what a buyer needs to see, imprint basics, an about-block pattern, before/after rewrites, mistakes and a block you can copy.

Why "who is behind this" matters

Before paying, a buyer asks a quiet question: if something goes wrong, who do I talk to, and can I find them? A page with no name, no address and only a contact form makes that question impossible to answer. The offer can be clear and the price fair, and the visitor still leaves because they can't tell whether a real business is on the other side.

Rule of thumb: a visitor should be able to name who they would be paying, and how to reach them, without leaving the page.

What a buyer needs to see

  • A real name or company name. Your own name if you trade as yourself, or the registered company name if you have one. If you use a brand name, show the legal name behind it too ("Brightline Studio is run by …").
  • Where you're based. At least the city and country; for the legal notice, a full postal address where required.
  • A contact that works. An email address in plain text (not only a form), answered in a timeframe you state. A phone number if you actually pick it up.
  • What you do and since when. One or two sentences on your background that relate to the offer. No life story.
  • A real photo, if you show one. Of you, not a model. No photo is better than a fake one.
  • Links to privacy and terms. If you collect personal data (a contact form, a newsletter, analytics), a privacy notice is expected; terms help when you sell directly.
  • How you use automation, if it matters. If an AI tool or an automated process does the work the buyer pays for, say so plainly.

Imprint basics (general information, not legal advice)

In the EU, businesses that offer services online are generally expected to make basic provider information easy to find: name, geographic address, contact details including an email address, and, where they apply, the company register entry and VAT number. In German-speaking countries this is usually called an Impressum, and for commercial websites in Germany and Austria it is often a legal requirement, with specific rules on what it must contain.

Requirements differ by country, legal form and type of business, and they change. Check the current rules for where you are based, or ask a lawyer or your chamber of commerce. This guide only covers what makes a page trustworthy to a buyer; it does not tell you what your legal obligations are.

Practical point: make the legal notice reachable from every page, usually via a footer link labelled "Imprint", "Legal notice" or "Impressum". Don't hide it behind an image or a script that search engines and screen readers can't read.

The about-block pattern

  1. Who: your name (and company name, if different), plus a real photo if you use one.
  2. Relevant background: one or two sentences that explain why you can deliver this offer.
  3. Where: city and country.
  4. How to reach you: a plain email address and when you reply.
  5. Where the details are: a link to the full legal notice, privacy and terms.

Five lines, close to the price or the main button. The full legal details belong in the footer or on a separate legal-notice page.

Before and after

Illustrative rewrites on made-up examples, not real people or businesses.

Freelance designer

Before: "We're a passionate team of creatives." (One person, stock photo of four people.) After: "I'm Jana Novak, a freelance brand designer in Ljubljana. I've designed logos and websites for small businesses since 2017. Questions: hello@example.com, I reply within one working day. Legal notice and privacy: see footer."

Bookkeeping service

Before: Contact form only, no name, no address. After: "Run by Ledgerly GmbH, Linz, Austria. Your contact is Thomas Berger, certified bookkeeper. Email: office@example.com. Company register number, VAT ID and full address in the Impressum."

One-person software studio

Before: "Brand X — innovative solutions worldwide." After: "Brand X is a one-person studio run by Sam Ortiz, based in Valencia, Spain. I build small internal tools for agencies. Email sam@example.com. Legal details on the legal-notice page."

Notice what changed: every "after" names a real-sounding person or entity, a place and a contact, and stops pretending to be bigger than it is.

Common mistakes

  • Stock photos posing as the team: buyers recognise them, and once they spot one they doubt the rest of the page.
  • "We" when it's one person: being a solo operator is not a weakness to hide. Many buyers like knowing exactly who does the work.
  • Fake or borrowed addresses: an address you don't actually use, or one that belongs to someone else, is misleading and, in a legal notice, can cause real trouble. A registered business address or a legitimate service address you're entitled to use is fine.
  • Hiding the owner: a brand name with no person or company behind it makes buyers wonder why.
  • Contact form as the only channel: people want to know where their message goes. Show an email address as well.
  • An inbox nobody reads: a listed address that bounces or never replies is worse than none.
  • Legal notice as an image or a broken link: it has to be readable and reachable.
  • Invented credentials: logos of clients you never had or certificates you don't hold. Show only what you can back up (see proof without testimonials).

Where it goes on the page

Put the short about block near the price or main button, because that's where the "who am I paying?" question comes up (see the price section guide and the call to action guide). Put the full legal details in the footer or on a linked legal-notice page, reachable from every page. If your "how it works" steps mention a reply from you, use the same name and email there (see the how it works guide).

Quick self-check

  • Can a visitor name who they would be paying?
  • Is there a city and country, and a full address in the legal notice where required?
  • Is there a plain email address that you actually answer?
  • Is every photo of the real person or team?
  • Is the legal notice reachable from every page as readable text?
  • Are privacy and terms linked?
